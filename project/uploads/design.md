Australian Seniors Property Advisors – Design Overview

This document captures the visual style and branding observed on the Australian Seniors Property Advisors (ASPA) website. It summarises the colour palette, typography choices, layout patterns and other design elements so that future work (for example, when re‑creating or extending the site) can remain consistent with the established brand.

Brand messaging

The ASPA home page immediately conveys the organisation’s value proposition. Large headings reassure visitors that ASPA will help them sell their home “for the maximum price, with minimum stress”. Supporting copy emphasises negotiation expertise and personalised service to take the stress out of selling. This tone should be maintained across all marketing materials: clear, supportive and focused on ease and trust.

Colour palette

The site relies on a muted yet professional palette dominated by deep blues and soft pastels. These colours communicate trust and calm while retaining enough contrast for clear call‑to‑action elements. Key colours defined in the site’s stylesheet include:

These colours should be reused consistently. When creating new components, derive lighter or darker tints from the existing palette instead of introducing untested hues.

Typography

Typography on the site balances elegance with readability. The CSS reveals that multiple fonts are loaded, including DM Sans, Metropolis and Roboto. In practice:

Headings – Large headings appear to use Metropolis or DM Sans with generous letter spacing. The weight is typically light or regular, giving an airy, modern feel.
Body copy – Paragraphs use Roboto at 16 px with a 25 px line height. This ensures legibility across devices.
Buttons and small labels – Button text and form labels utilise sans‑serif fonts (often Helvetica or Roboto) in uppercase or small caps. Rounded corners and generous padding enhance tap targets on mobile.

When drafting new pages, continue to use these typefaces. Maintain consistent line heights and letter‑spacing to preserve the site’s airy appearance.

Layout and imagery

The layout favours generous margins and structured sections. Notable patterns include:

Hero sections – Each major message is supported by a full‑width background image (for example, older adults enjoying life) with a semi‑opaque overlay to ensure white text remains legible. Headlines and call‑to‑action buttons are centred in this space.
Two‑column splits – Informational sections alternate text and imagery, often placing a photograph on one side and copy on the other. Images are sometimes framed by subtle pastel shapes or rounded rectangles, giving depth and visual interest.
Call to action – The “Let’s Talk” button is prominent and uses the primary light blue colour for visibility. It appears multiple times down the page to encourage contact.
Whitespace – Generous whitespace between sections helps prevent cognitive overload and guides the eye down the page.
User interface elements
Navigation – The top navigation bar is simple, with a dark background and light text. On smaller screens it collapses into a hamburger menu. A banner at the top offers a downloadable guide using contrasting colours to attract attention.
Buttons – Buttons are pill‑shaped with rounded corners and solid fills. Primary actions use the light blue, while secondary actions may use a transparent outline.
Forms – Contact forms are minimalistic, using clear labels and generous input fields. Form validation feedback appears inline, maintaining the page’s neat appearance.
Footer – The footer uses a dark background and organises information into three columns (contact details, office hours, and social links). Body copy remains light‑coloured for contrast.
Imagery and iconography

Photographs throughout the site feature seniors in positive, aspirational contexts (e.g., enjoying travel or leisure). Images are high‑resolution and lightly edited to blend into the page’s colour palette. Icons (where used) follow a simple line style to complement the typography. Avoid stock images that feel generic; choose photos that convey authenticity and warmth.

Motion and interactions

Subtle animations (hover effects and scroll‑triggered transitions) add polish without distracting from content. For example, buttons gently change shade on hover, and sections may fade into view as the user scrolls. When adding new interactions, favour smooth transitions over flashy animations.

Summary and recommendations

The ASPA website communicates trust and expertise through its restrained colour palette, modern typography and spacious layouts. When extending or redesigning the site, adhere to the existing palette and font choices, maintain generous whitespace, and incorporate imagery that reflects the target demographic. By following these guidelines, new pages or components will blend seamlessly with the existing site while supporting the organisation’s brand message of achieving the best price with minimal stress
