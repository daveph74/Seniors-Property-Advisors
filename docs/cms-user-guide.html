Using the CMS — a starter guide

For the people who sign in and keep the Seniors Property Advisors website up to date. It covers your first hour: signing in, finding your way around, changing a page and putting it live, and the handful of rules that are not obvious from looking at the screen.

Everything here describes what is actually on the screen. Where a button is named in bold, that is the wording you are looking for.

A few things in the CMS are reserved for whoever looks after the site for you, and this guide does not cover them. If your sidebar is shorter than a colleague's, or a button answers that you are not allowed, that is how the CMS is set up rather than something going wrong.

Before you start

The website has two halves.

  • The public website is what visitors see.
  • The CMS is where you change it. You reach it by adding /cms to the end of the website address.

You need an account. Whoever looks after the site sets one up for you and gives you a password to start with.

Your first five minutes

1. Sign in

Go to /login. Enter your Email address and Password, then Sign in.

  • Keep me signed in on this device is worth ticking on your own computer, and worth leaving alone on a shared one.
  • Five wrong passwords in a row pauses sign-in for a few minutes. Wait it out rather than trying harder — the pause is per account and per computer.

2. Change the password you were given

Once you are in, an amber strip sits across the top of every screen:

Change your password. This account is still using the password it was given, which somebody else chose and may still know. Set your own password.

It does not go away when you dismiss it, because there is nothing to dismiss. The way to clear it is to change the password — that is the entire point of it.

Click Set your own password (or go to Your account at the bottom of the sidebar). Fill in Current password, New password and Confirm new password.

A checklist appears under the box as you type and ticks itself off:

  • At least 10 characters long
  • At least one uppercase letter
  • At least one lowercase letter
  • At least one number
  • At least one special character

One more rule is not on that list, because it is checked when you save rather than as you type: a password already known to have been leaked in somebody else's data breach is refused, however strong it looks. If a password with five green ticks is turned away, that is why — choose a different one.

Saving it signs you out on every other device — your phone, another browser, an office computer you forgot about. That is deliberate. Sign back in on those when you next need them.

3. Look around

The sidebar down the left is the whole CMS. Along the top:

  • Search pages, articles, media… — press it, or press Ctrl+K (⌘K on a Mac). Start typing and it finds pages, articles, images, questions, testimonials and enquiries, and takes you straight to them. Two letters is the minimum.
  • The bell — how many enquiries nobody has opened yet.
  • Preview site — takes you to the public website, so you can see what a visitor sees. Use the browser's back button to return.

At the bottom of the sidebar are Your account and Sign out.

The one rule that explains everything

Saving is not publishing.

Every page has two versions at once: the one visitors are reading, and the one you are working on.

What you press What happens
Save draft Your work is kept. The website does not change. You can close the browser and come back to it.
Preview Shows you your draft in a new tab, exactly as it will look. Only you can see it.
Publish Replaces what visitors see with your draft. This is the only button that changes the public website.

A page you have saved but not published shows Unpublished changes in the pages list, and the number beside Pages in the sidebar counts how many pages are in that state — it is a reminder list of work you started and have not finished.

Articles work slightly differently, and that is covered in the article walkthrough below.

1Walkthroughchange a page and put it live

Pages in the sidebar.

You get a list of every page, with its Status, how many Sections it has, when it was Last updated and by whom. The List / Site tree buttons switch between a flat list and the same pages arranged the way the website nests them.

  1. Find the page and press Edit page. The builder opens.
  2. The builder has three parts:
    • Components on the left — the blocks you can add, grouped as Layout, Text, Media, Lists & grids, Badges & callouts, and Website sections. Layers beside it shows the Page structure as a list, which is the easier way to find something on a long page.
    • The canvas in the middle — the page itself. Click a block to select it.
    • The settings panel on the right — every setting for whatever you have selected, in accordions: Content is already open, and Layout, Style, Responsive and Advanced open when you click their heading.
  3. Click the text you want to change, and edit it in the panel on the right.
  4. To add something, drag it from Components onto the page.
  5. Hovering a block gives you a small toolbar: move it, duplicate it, save it as a reusable section, Hide on page, or delete it. Hiding is not deleting — the block stays, and stops being shown.
  6. Undo and Redo are at the top left, next to the Desktop / Tablet / Mobile buttons. Those three change what the canvas shows you; they do not change the page.

Above the canvas it says All changes saved or Unsaved changes, so you always know where you are.

  1. Press Save draft. It says Draft saved.
  2. Press Preview. If you have unsaved work it asks first — You have unsaved changes — and offers to save and preview, or to Preview last saved.
  3. When you are happy, press Publish. A dialog opens showing the page, its address, its current status and Changes being published — a plain list of what is actually different from what is live. Read it; it is the last chance to notice you changed something you did not mean to.
  4. Confirm. It says [Page name] is now live.

Going back to an earlier version

History at the top of the builder opens a list of every time the page was published. Each version offers Preview, Compare with live and Restore. Restoring may not be something your account is allowed to do; if it answers that you are not allowed, ask whoever looks after the site.

Restoring does not put the old version straight onto the website. It becomes your draft, so you can look at it, change your mind, and publish it only when you mean to.

From the list, without opening the builder

The three-dots button at the end of each row of the pages list opens a menu: Edit page, Preview, View history, Duplicate, Publish changes, Unpublish and Archive page.

  • Unpublish takes the page off the public website. Nothing is lost.
  • Archive page puts it out of the way. It comes back from the Archived filter, though bringing one back may not be something your account is allowed to do.

2Walkthroughcreate a new page

Pages → New page, top right. The Create a page dialog asks for four things:

  • Page name — what it is called. For example, Preparing to Move.
  • Web address — filled in for you from the name. This is what visitors will see in their browser.
  • Sits under — leave it alone for a top-level page, or choose a parent to nest it beneath.
  • Start from — a starting layout: blank, or one already arranged as a standard page, a service page, a landing page or a blog listing. It is only a starting point; everything in it can be changed or deleted.

Create it and the builder opens on the new page.

A new page is not on the website until you publish it. You can leave it half-finished for a week and no visitor will ever find it.

Renaming a page that is already live

You can change a page's Web address in the builder, under Page settings. If the page has been published, anyone following the old link is sent to the new address automatically, so links in emails, newsletters and search results keep working. If the page was never published there is nothing to preserve, so nothing is kept.

The home page cannot be renamed.

3Walkthroughwrite an article

Blog in the sidebar lists every article with its status. New article starts a new one.

The editor is in two halves. On the left, the article itself: Title, Web address (made from the title if you leave it), Short summary — the blurb under the headline on the blog listing — and the body, which you write the way you would write in a word processor. The toolbar gives you headings, bold, italic, lists, links, quotes and images.

On the right:

  • Details — Author name, Published date, Featured image and Categories.
  • Search and sharing — SEO title, SEO description, a Social sharing image, a Canonical address and Hide from search engines.

Press Create article (or Save on an existing one), then Preview to see it as a reader will, then Publish.

Three things worth knowing:

  • Published date is the date readers see. It does not schedule anything. Setting it to next Tuesday does not publish it on Tuesday — it publishes when you press Publish, and then says Tuesday on it.
  • Editing a published article changes it immediately. Articles have no separate draft the way pages do. If you need to work on a live article at length, Unpublish it first, or write it fresh and publish when it is finished.
  • Pasting an article from somewhere else keeps your words and drops any pictures that live on another website. A message tells you how many were removed. Add the pictures again from the image button in the toolbar, which opens the media library.

Categories are managed from the blog list: Add a category, then press Enter. Deleting a category keeps its articles — anything left without one moves to Uncategorised.

4Walkthroughadd a question to the FAQs

FAQs in the sidebar. Add a question opens a small form:

  • Question and Answer. The answer is plain text, one or two paragraphs.
  • Category — optional, and managed on the same screen under Categories.
  • Show only on this page — the one that catches people. Leave it empty and the question appears wherever FAQs are shown on the site. Put a page's address in it and the question belongs to that page and appears nowhere else.

Drag the questions into the order you want them read; the order is saved as you drop them, and the whole point of the screen is that the most-asked question sits at the top.

The switch at the end of a row takes a question off the website without deleting it; the row then shows a Hidden badge. Categories have the same switch, and hiding a category hides the category, not its questions.

Questions appear on the public site wherever a page has an FAQ section in it. Adding a question does not add it to a page that has no such section.

5Walkthroughadd a testimonial

Testimonials in the sidebar, then Add a testimonial: Client name, Location or suburb, a Short heading, the Testimonial itself, a Rating and a Client photo.

Then the part that surprises everybody:

A testimonial does not appear on the website until you record the client's permission.

A new testimonial shows Permission needed and sits under Waiting on permission. Press Record permission, confirm, and it changes to Permission recorded. The CMS keeps who confirmed it and when.

Two switches control where it appears, and they only do anything once permission is recorded:

  • Showing on the website — whether it is used at all.
  • Featured on the home page — whether it is one of the highlighted few.

Withdraw permission is there for when a client changes their mind. It takes the testimonial off the website straight away and unfeatures it in the same move. You do not have to remember to also turn the switches off.

Drag to reorder, the same as the FAQs.

6Walkthroughdeal with an enquiry

Enquiries in the sidebar. Two kinds of enquiry arrive here, and the tabs across the top separate them: All, Contact form and Agent Finder.

Beside the tabs, a dropdown chooses Waiting for a reply, Already dealt with or Everything, and there is a box to search a name, email, suburb or message.

Click a row to open it. You get:

  • who wrote in, when, which form it came from, which page they were on, and — for Agent Finder — the reference they were given, such as AF-2026-00042.
  • their email address and phone number as links, so replying is a click.
  • for an Agent Finder enquiry, the answers they picked from the four questions, then In their own words above anything they typed.
  • Where has this got to? — set it to New, In progress or Dealt with. Underneath it says Only you can see this. Nothing here is sent to the person who wrote in.

Reply by email opens your email program with their address already filled in. Replies are sent from your own email, not from the CMS — the CMS does not send email at all, and nothing you do on this screen is seen by the person who wrote in.

Nothing on this screen can be edited. The name, the message and the answers are the sender's own words, so there is deliberately no way to change them.

Two counts that mean different things

This confuses people, so it is worth being clear:

  • The bell at the top counts enquiries nobody has opened. Opening one clears it. It can honestly reach zero.
  • The number beside Enquiries in the sidebar counts enquiries still waiting for a reply. Reading one does not change it. Only marking it Dealt with does.

Glancing at something is not the same as doing it, which is why the two numbers disagree on purpose.

The inbox does not update itself while you sit on it. Reload the page and everything that has arrived since is there.

7Walkthroughupload an image and put it on a page

Getting the image in

Media in the sidebar. Drag files onto the page, or press Upload.

Click an uploaded image and fill in two boxes:

  • Describe the image — read aloud to visitors who cannot see it, and used by search engines. Say what is in the picture: Advisor guiding a senior couple. Worth doing on every image; a great many of our visitors use a screen reader.
  • Caption — optional, and shown under the image on the page.

Address on that panel is where the image lives. You do not need to copy it anywhere — as the hint says, To put it on a page, use Choose on the block itself.

Putting it on a page

In the builder, select an image block. The panel on the right has a Choose button (Replace once there is an image) which opens the media library. Pick one, or drop a new file straight onto the block.

There is nothing to type. You cannot paste an image address from another website — pictures have to live on this site, or they will not be shown to readers at all.

Deleting images

An image that is still used on a page, an article or a testimonial cannot be deleted. Try it and the CMS tells you what is still using it. Take it off those first.

Deleting an image that genuinely is unused is permanent — the file is removed from storage. This is one of the few things in the CMS that cannot be undone, and not every account is allowed to do it.

8Walkthroughthe menus, and the wording on every page

Two screens change things that appear on every page, so a change here is seen everywhere at once.

Navigation has three lists:

  • Header menu — the links across the top. A link can be set to Opens a dropdown, in which case the links under it become the dropdown.
  • Footer columns — the grouped links at the bottom.
  • Small print — the row underneath those.

Drag to reorder, then Save menus. Nothing changes on the website until you press it.

Global content holds the wording around the edges:

  • Header — the logo, the Name beside the logo, and what to Describe the logo as.
  • Announcement bar — the strip across the top of the site. Show the announcement bar, the Wording, Button wording and Where it goes. Turn it off when the announcement is over.
  • Footer — the Short description, Phone number, Address and Copyright line.

The phone number and address are stored here and nowhere else, so this is the one place to change them.

9Walkthroughcheck how a page looks in a search result

SEO in the sidebar. Two tabs: Overview and Defaults.

Overview is one row per address on the public website — every page and every article. The columns tell you what a search engine actually receives:

  • Address — the public address, and a link to edit it.
  • Search title — the title in a search result. It gets | Seniors Property Advisors added to the end, and the row warns you when the whole thing is Long for a search result or Very short.
  • Search description — the sentence under the title. It is flagged when it is Using the site default, which is worth fixing: a sentence shared by a dozen addresses tells a reader nothing about which one to open.
  • In sitemap — whether search engines are being told this address exists.
  • Changed — when it last changed.

Two things can be edited straight from a row: Search description and Hide from search engines. Everything else is edited in the builder's Page settings, or in an article's Search and sharing panel.

Two lengths are worth respecting, because Google cuts the rest off mid-sentence:

  • Descriptions: about 155 characters.
  • Titles: about 61 characters, including the | Seniors Property Advisors on the end.

Defaults sets the Title pattern, Default description and Default sharing image used by any address that has not been given its own. Treat them as a safety net, not as the answer.

A deleted article still appears in the list — its address may still be sitting in somebody's search results, which is exactly what you would come here to explain — but it cannot be edited. It has to be brought back before it can be changed, which is something to ask whoever looks after the site for.

Keeping an eye on things

Dashboard is the first screen you land on. It shows what is Waiting to be published, what was Recently edited and Recently published, and Recent activity. Everything on it is counted at the moment you open it.

Activity is the full record: who changed what, and when. Every change made anywhere in the CMS is recorded against the person signed in, including publishing, unpublishing, archiving and deleting. Nothing on this screen can be edited, by anybody.

Nine things that surprise people

  1. Saving is not publishing. Save draft keeps your work; only Publish changes the website.
  2. A page that has never been published is invisible. Creating it is not enough, and neither is linking to it from a menu.
  3. A testimonial shows nothing until permission is recorded — not the quote, not the name, not the photo.
  4. Deleting content is recoverable. A deleted article, question or testimonial is not destroyed, and can be brought back. Deleting an unused image is the exception — that one is final.
  5. Renaming a live page keeps the old link working. Anyone following the old address is sent to the new one.
  6. Enquiries cannot be edited. The words are the sender's, and there is no way to change them.
  7. The bell and the sidebar number count different things. The bell is what nobody has opened; the sidebar is what nobody has dealt with.
  8. An article's published date does not schedule it. It publishes when you press Publish.
  9. An image in use cannot be deleted, and the CMS will tell you what is still using it.

If something looks wrong

  • A change is not showing on the website. Nine times in ten it was saved and not published. Open the page and look for Unpublished changes.
  • You cannot find a screen this guide mentions. Check the table above for its address. A sidebar with fewer entries than a colleague's is how your account is set up, not a fault.
  • A button refuses and explains why. Read it. The CMS is deliberate about refusing rather than quietly doing something surprising — an image still in use, a page you may not restore, a password that is not strong enough.
  • Anything else — ask whoever looks after the site for you.
